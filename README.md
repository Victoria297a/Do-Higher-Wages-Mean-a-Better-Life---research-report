# Do Higher Wages Mean a Better Life?

### Exploring Regional Well-Being Across Europe with AI & Eurostat Data

<p align="center">
  <strong>Can higher wages actually translate into a healthier, less stressful, and longer life?</strong>
</p>

---

## 📌 About the Project

Across Europe, wages vary substantially between regions. A common assumption is that **higher income automatically leads to a better quality of life**.

But is that really the case?

This project investigates the relationship between **regional wages and broader measures of well-being** across Europe. Rather than defining prosperity purely through income, we examine whether higher wages are associated with:

* 🏃 **Physical activity**
* 🧠 **Stress and mental well-being**
* ❤️ **Life expectancy**
* 💰 **Regional wages**

The analysis uses **Eurostat regional data** and AI-assisted research methods to explore patterns, identify relationships, and challenge initial assumptions.

The project was developed as part of the **Modul & MCC AI Workshop Weekend Academy**.

---

# 🎯 Research Question

> **Do higher wages mean a better life?**

More specifically:

> **Are higher regional wages associated with healthier lifestyles, lower stress levels, and longer life expectancy across European regions?**

The purpose is not to establish a single causal relationship, but to explore whether economic prosperity and quality of life move together — and where they diverge.

---

# 💡 Research Motivation

Income is one of the most widely used indicators of economic prosperity.

However, income alone does not capture the full experience of living in a region.

Two regions can have very different wage levels while their populations experience:

* different health outcomes,
* different levels of physical activity,
* different levels of psychological stress,
* and different life expectancies.

This raises a broader question:

> **Should a region be considered more prosperous simply because its residents earn more?**

This project approaches well-being as a **multidimensional concept**, combining economic and health-related indicators.

---

# 📊 Data

The primary data source is **Eurostat**, using regional European statistics where available.

The analysis focuses on four main dimensions:

| Dimension            | Indicator         | Purpose                            |
| -------------------- | ----------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| 💰 Economic          | Regional wages    | Measure economic prosperity        |
| 🏃 Lifestyle         | Physical activity | Measure healthy lifestyle behavior |
| 🧠 Mental well-being | Stress            | Explore psychological well-being   |
| ❤️ Health            | Life expectancy   | Measure long-term health outcomes  |

The regional level of analysis is based on the **NUTS (Nomenclature of Territorial Units for Statistics)** framework.

Using regional rather than purely national data allows us to investigate differences that can otherwise be hidden by country-level averages.

# 🤖 How AI Was Used

AI was incorporated throughout the research process.

### 1. Research Exploration

AI was used to brainstorm possible explanations for why wages may or may not correspond to better living conditions.

Potential dimensions considered included:

* health,
* lifestyle,
* housing,
* unemployment,
* inequality,
* stress,
* and public services.

### 2. Indicator Selection

AI-assisted exploration helped compare potential indicators and identify variables that could complement wage data.

The final analysis focuses on:

**Wages → Physical Activity → Stress → Life Expectancy**

### 3. Data Analysis

AI-assisted tools can support:

* data exploration,
* identification of patterns,
* correlation analysis,
* outlier detection,
* and visualization design.

### 4. Interpretation

AI was used as a research assistant rather than as an authority.

Potential explanations generated through AI were treated as **hypotheses requiring human evaluation**, rather than automatically accepted conclusions.

# 🧭 Interpreting the Results

The central hypothesis is intentionally simple:

> **Higher wages → better life**

However, the research investigates whether the real relationship is more complex.

A region with high wages may also experience:

* high living pressure,
* elevated stress,
* sedentary employment,
* or other factors that influence well-being.

Conversely, a region with lower wages may perform relatively well on certain health or lifestyle indicators.

Therefore, the project treats **well-being as multidimensional rather than purely economic**.

---

# ⚠️ Important: Correlation ≠ Causation

One of the most important limitations of this research is that regional correlations cannot demonstrate that wages **cause** differences in health or well-being.

For example:

> A region with higher wages and higher life expectancy does not necessarily mean that higher wages caused people to live longer.

Other factors may contribute, including:

* healthcare systems,
* education,
* demographics,
* employment structure,
* environmental conditions,
* cultural differences,
* public infrastructure,
* and historical regional development.

The findings should therefore be interpreted as **associations and patterns**, not causal effects.

---

# 🧩 Limitations

Several limitations should be considered.

### Data availability

Not every indicator is necessarily available at exactly the same regional level or for exactly the same year.

### Regional comparability

European regions differ considerably in:

* demographic structure,
* economic systems,
* healthcare,
* culture,
* urbanization,
* and labor markets.

### Measurement differences

Some indicators, particularly stress and subjective well-being, may rely on self-reported information.

### Aggregation

Regional averages can hide substantial differences between individuals within the same region.

### AI limitations

AI-generated interpretations can contain:

* oversimplifications,
* incorrect assumptions,
* or unsupported causal explanations.

For this reason, AI outputs were treated as **research support**, not as a replacement for statistical or economic reasoning.

---

# 🌍 Why This Matters

Economic development is often evaluated through indicators such as income, GDP, and employment.

But people do not experience economic development through statistics alone.

They experience it through:

> **How much they earn → how they live → how healthy they are → how stressed they feel → and how long they live.**

This project therefore asks whether a broader definition of prosperity is needed when evaluating European regions.

---

# 🧠 Key Takeaway

> **Higher wages may contribute to a better life, but income alone does not define well-being.**

A meaningful assessment of regional prosperity should consider both **economic resources and human outcomes**.

The relationship between wages, health, lifestyle, and well-being is therefore better understood as **multidimensional rather than linear**.
